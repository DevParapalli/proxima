v0.3.0 · built for one person

Dark first, quiet until it matters.

This is the design system behind my homelab console. Dark is the default because that is what I prefer, but the system ships four accents, two themes and two backgrounds, and every combination is a real supported option. Everything below is live. Use the panel in the bottom right to switch, and watch the components and the swatches follow along.

01Principles

Six rules, and one thing that is up to you

The rules below hold whichever look you pick. What you pick is your call. Indigo on ink dark is only the default. Teal on flat light is just as valid, and if that suits the thing you are building, use it. Sixteen combinations, all of them checked.

01

Neutrals come from the ramp

Never #000, never #fff, never untinted grey. On dark the ramp leans blue, on light it stays cool. Both feel like a place rather than an inverted settings panel.

02

One accent, your pick of four

Indigo, teal, ember or lime. Choose one at the root and let it own active states, primary buttons and focus rings. The rule is one accent per app, not one accent for everyone.

03

State twice over

Status is a tint you feel and a word you read. Never colour on its own, never a bare label. This is what keeps the palette swappable without losing meaning.

04

Depth from glass, or not at all

Things lift with blur and translucency instead of shadows, and only the top layer gets a real shadow. Flat mode drops the blooms and the system still works.

05

Hide the detail

A row is one line until you open it. Density is something you ask for, not something the page does to you.

06

Texture, barely

Five percent grain, soft colour blooms underneath, both optional. Atmosphere is fine. Noise is not.

rule: pick a combination, set it once at the root, and stay there. Every rule above survives the switch. Your taste decides which of the sixteen you ship.

03Type

One family for every heading

Outfit sets all text and every heading. Heading sizes follow one rule: each level down divides by the square root of the golden ratio, so two levels make one factor of φ, and h6 is body size. IBM Plex Mono handles labels, hostnames, timestamps and anything a machine produced. Instrument Serif is kept for quotations and the drop cap that opens a long article. 08Responsive

The navigation goes off canvas, nothing else disappears

A phone gets the same pages, not a reduced set. Grids collapse on a fixed ladder, the app sidebar and this rail slide in from the left rather than vanishing, and every chart stays readable and inspectable with a finger.

1 · The ladder

Six widths, each owned by a single decision. If a change does not belong to one of these, it does not happen.
WidthWhat changesWhy
1140.settings-cols single column, .subnav hidesthe "on this page" list stops earning its width
1100.mosaic spans halvetwelve columns stop reading as twelve
980.cols-main, .panes and the marketing embed go single columntwo columns of data stop fitting side by side
880.side and .rail become drawers, .navtoggle and .brand-sm appearthe app shell loses its sidebar column
720nav.top .links becomes a dropdown panelthe marketing links stop fitting beside the logo
620table rows restack, comparison bars narrow, frame padding tightensthe last width where two columns of anything work
rule: a collapsed grid column is minmax(0, 1fr), never 1fr. A bare 1fr is minmax(auto, 1fr) and cannot shrink below its content, which is how a chart ends up half off the screen.

2 · The drawer, .navtoggle and data-nav-toggle

One button, one attribute, whose value is a selector for the panel it opens. Everything else is wired for you: the shared scrim, focus moved into the drawer and trapped there, Escape to close and return focus to the button, a tap on any link inside to close, and a forced close when the viewport goes back above the breakpoint. Add data-nav-breakpoint when the panel collapses somewhere other than 880, as the marketing pages do at 720.
<button class="navtoggle" data-nav-toggle="#app-nav">
Narrow this window past 880 and the live one appears in the top left, over the rail it opens. On the console pages it sits at the head of the topbar instead, because there is already a bar to put it in.
rule: a closed drawer is visibility: hidden, not display: none. Hidden keeps its links out of the tab order, which display would also do, but it still allows the panel to slide.

3 · Charts under a finger

A spark chart measures its own box and sets its viewBox so that one user unit is one CSS pixel. Nothing is scaled unevenly, which is what keeps the dotted forecast dotted at 390px wide instead of thinning into blank space. Press and drag the chart above for the crosshair and the readout, and let go to dismiss it. The chart claims sideways drags and leaves vertical ones to the page, so scrolling over it still works.
rule: never set preserveAspectRatio="none" on a chart. It buys a chart that fills its box and costs you every stroke width, dash and radius in it.

4 · The background on a small screen

The blooms are placed as fractions of the canvas box rather than at fixed coordinates, so they stay round on a tall narrow screen instead of smearing into vertical bands. Below 880 the blur softens from 90px to 60px, and the grain drops its blend mode, which removes a full screen compositing layer and the banding it used to show over flat mode. Flat itself now stops painting and animating the ink layer altogether rather than leaving it running at zero opacity.
rule: flat is a real mode, not a hidden one. If the switch says the atmosphere is off, nothing about it should still be costing the device work.
